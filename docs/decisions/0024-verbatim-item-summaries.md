# 0024: Verbatim item summaries from requested actions and staff reports

Status: accepted (the verbatim rule and the requested-action rule are maintainer instructions; the 2026-11-02 validator, prompt, partial-summary, and reading-view changes were approved by the maintainer); the maintainer chose on 2026-11-02 to keep the Sonnet fallback after the measured re-run; the 2026-11-03 validator and prompt changes were maintainer decisions, and the maintainer replaced the Sonnet fallback with Opus 5.5 at low effort after the measurement of 2026-11-03; proposed default for the rest
Date: 2026-10-27
Related ideas: IDEA-013, IDEA-016
Related decisions: 0004, 0010, 0011, 0015, 0020

## Context

An expanded agenda item showed only an agenda excerpt, which usually repeated the title (IDEA-016). The fuller account is in the item's requested action, which many agendas print under the item, and in its staff report or executive summary, which agencies publish separately or inside the agenda packet (IDEA-013).

## Decision

**Accepted maintainer instructions (2026-10-27):**

- **A summary repeats the source verbatim as much as possible**, changing it only where needed for brevity. Sentences are selected from the agenda and the staff report and trimmed; nothing is paraphrased, characterized, or added.
- **The summary leads with the item's requested or recommended action, verbatim, whenever the agenda states one** ("Requested Action / Suggested Motions:", "Recommended Action:", "Recommendation:", "Requested Action:", "Action Requested:"). An item with a printed requested action but no mapped staff report still gets a summary from that text alone. A TTD-style "For Possible Action:" line is the item's title and is not repeated.
- **Show links to every attachment the agenda lists under an item**, labeled from the printed file name, with the printed name kept for matching. No file type is shown: printed extensions are unreliable (South Lake Tahoe serves its .docx attachments as PDFs), and the maintainer found the labels not useful (2026-10-27).

**Proposed defaults (this implementation):**

1. *Staff report links* (`tahoe-ingest map-staff-reports`, `tahoe_ingest/staff_reports.py`). One rule per agency, each requiring two independent signals to agree:
   - South Lake Tahoe: the agenda PDF's "Item N- Executive Summary.pdf" link, and that file's first page reading "Agenda Item #:N" with the item's title and meeting date.
   - TTD: the agenda's Page column, the packet's "~ Page N ~" stamp on that page, and the board's "AGENDA ITEM: N." footer on it.
   - Truckee: the agenda packet (stored, or Municode's MEET-Packet file beside the captured agenda PDF, which must open with that agenda), with a single run of pages headed "Item N.N" whose first page carries the item's title.
   - Lahontan: the agenda's `itemN.pdf` link, headed "ITEM N" with the item's title and meeting.
   Uncertain items store nothing. Only the URL, page range, hash, evidence, and the text of the report's first three pages (for summary citations) are kept; the PDFs are temporary (decision 0004). The meeting page shows "Read the staff report" (or "Read the executive summary") beside "Read in the agenda".
2. *Attachment lists* (South Lake Tahoe). Every link between an item's "N." heading and the next left-margin heading of the agenda PDF belongs to that item, in agenda order. Each printed name must also appear on that page of the stored agenda text, and an executive summary numbered for another item makes the whole list uncertain. URL, printed name, and readable label are stored, never the files.
3. *Requested actions* (`tahoe_ingest/requested_actions.py`, no model). The label must start a line between the item's cited title and the next item's in the same agenda; the action must be one complete statement ending in a period, 5–120 words. It is the exact page text, cited to its page, and is computed at export time from stored text (no extra queries).
4. *Staff report sentences* (`tahoe-ingest summarize-items`, `tahoe_ingest/item_summaries.py`, prompt `prompts/staff_report_summary.md`). Haiku 5.5 at low effort selects one to three sentences (one or two after a printed requested action), each with the cited page and the passage it trimmed. It leads with what is decided or presented when the report says so in at least five words, and never uses procedural boilerplate ("Receive and file", "For presentation only", "No financial impact") as a sentence; for a proclamation, award, or presentation it uses the sentence that says what is recognized (2026-11-02, replacing the rule that always led with the recommendation). A rejected answer is retried with the rejected sentence, its source, the page text near it, and a fix for that kind of rejection; Haiku gets a second try, then Opus 5.5 at low effort one (Sonnet 5.5 until 2026-11-03). Procedural items (roll call, minutes, adjournment, public comment, closed session) are skipped.
5. *Validation before publication* (decision 0010; no human approval; rules as of 2026-11-03 in the [update below](#update-2026-11-03-validator-3-and-prompt-p1)). Each passage must be found on its cited page (words compared, so line breaks and misread quote characters do not matter), and each sentence must be that passage with words deleted: at most one inserted "the", "a", "an", or "and" per ten words, no new words or numbers, at most three cuts from the middle of a passage of at most 12 words each, and no dropped negation within or next to the kept words. Each passage must begin where a sentence begins on its page, and a sentence may drop only whole sentences before it, an opening clause ending in a comma or colon, or a list marker such as "(1)", so every published sentence is a whole sentence of the source; cutting the end is free. A source must have 5 to 160 words, and a short or long one is reported as such. The first sentence must pass or nothing is published and the item keeps its agenda text; a later sentence that fails is left out and the rest published (2026-11-02; before, one failing sentence failed the summary). Failed and skipped inputs are recorded and not sent again until something the model sees changes. Each rejected reply is stored with its usage row (`ai_usage.error`, after `reply:`), in the same write, so validator changes can be checked offline. Each result records `VALIDATOR_VERSION` (`item_summaries.py`); raising it after loosening a rule sends the items that failed validation once more, and only those, where a prompt change would send every item. Each run reports why its items are pending (`itemsPendingByReason`): `new`, `prompt`, `report` (the staff report changed), `agenda` (the item was revised), `other` (another part of the message, such as a title or the requested action), `validator`, or `forced`.
   *Reading view* (`reading_view.py`, 2026-11-02). Model and validator read each page in reading order rather than as `pdftotext -layout` prints it: on a form page, labels printed in a side column (the Nevada water board's "System Infrastructure", "for the project") move onto their own lines out of the sentences beside them, and a word hyphenated across a line break is joined when the report prints the joined word elsewhere. Stored page text is not rewritten; a citation quotes the stored page's own span covering the passage (labels moved out of it included), so the export's quote check, the website, staff report mapping, and source fingerprints are unaffected. The label rule is deliberately narrow (a body column at least eight characters in, short labels to its left whose body runs on at that column, and at least two such blocks or three labels on the page); on local data it changes 11 of 1,697 stored report pages (Nevada water board forms, Lahontan timelines, one transit table). Labels printed flush against the body with no consistent column (some NDEP project narratives) are not separated.
6. *Precedence and invalidation.* The export shows the staff report summary while it matches the item's source fingerprint and its current staff report hash; otherwise the agenda's requested action; otherwise the agenda excerpt. A revised agenda item changes the fingerprint, so a stale summary is never shown. Since [decision 0034](0034-fingerprints-without-document-versions.md) the fingerprint leaves out the document version, so an agenda posted again as a new file with the same text keeps every fingerprint, and ingest points the summary's requested-action citation at the new version (or hides the summary when that quote is not on the new page). When a fingerprint does change but what the model is sent does not (a re-flowed title quote), `summarize-items` rebinds the stored result to the current fingerprint, and its requested-action citation to the current agenda version, in one statement and without a Claude request (`itemsRebound` in the run summary). Operator corrections and suppression (`editorial`) apply on top and survive reprocessing. Published summaries are also appended to `summary_versions` (internal audit).
7. *Budget.* Summaries have their own per-run limit (`--summary-max-cost`, default $0.50) under the shared $5 monthly AI budget; a run takes as many pending items as fit and leaves the rest for the next. At most 6 meetings are scanned for staff reports per run (`--staff-report-meetings`), nearest to today first. Mapping writes one statement per scanned meeting and summaries one per meeting; the export reads all of it in one query (13 statements in total, still fixed).

## Measurements (local data, 2026-10-27)

- Staff report links: 135 items in 18 meetings (South Lake Tahoe 60, TTD 36, Truckee 21, Lahontan 18). SLT attachment lists: 75 items, 364 files; one item was left out because a wrapped file name did not match the agenda text.
- Summaries: 117 from staff reports (SLT 53, TTD 30, Truckee 19, Lahontan 15), 42 of them led by the agenda's printed requested action; 40 more items show the agenda's requested action alone (SLT City Council 17, Planning Commission 10, Parks and Recreation Commission 4, Truckee 9). TTD's agenda lines are "For Possible Action:" titles, so its actions come from the staff memos.
- Validation, final rules: of 135 items, 117 published, 8 were skipped by the model (enclosure covers, calendars, or nothing beyond the requested action), and 10 failed validation and kept their agenda text (7%). Before the whole-sentence rule, 1 of 135 failed, but several published sentences were verbatim fragments.
- Cost: about $0.0015 per item with Haiku 5.5 (Sonnet 5.5 fallbacks included). These four agencies add roughly 60–150 items a month: $0.10–0.25 a month.
- Database statements per scheduled run: about 4 reads for each of the two steps, plus about 2 per meeting scanned (at most 6) and 1 per meeting summarized; about 15 on an ordinary run, under 2,000 a month (1% of the free quota).

## Measurements (local data, 2026-11-02)

- Under the rules of 2026-10-27, local runs on 2026-10-28 and 2026-10-29 tried 838 items: Haiku's first answer passed for 616 (74%), its retry rescued 102 of 222 (46%), Sonnet 25 of 120 (21%), and 95 failed (11.3%; 90 of 778 stored items). Failed calls cost $1.02 of $1.78; Sonnet's 120 calls cost $0.90 (half the spend) for 25 summaries. Retries mostly repeated the same rejection, because most rejections were the validator's, not the model's.
- Causes, each reproduced against stored page text: a source under five words ("Action: Receive presentation", "For presentation only.", "No financial impact.") was reported as "not on page"; a passage opening with "$", "(", or a quotation mark could never start a sentence (no published summary had a sentence starting with "$"); only a passage's first occurrence was tried, so a recommended motion that repeats the item's title (every Douglas County cover page) was judged mid-sentence; numbered motions ("3)"), Word's Symbol-font bullet, and "o" sub-bullets did not start a passage; dropping a whole earlier sentence was refused; "Project No. 0184" and a following "No action is required." counted as dropped negations; side-column labels interleaved into sentences (Nevada water board forms).
- After the changes, all 670 published summaries whose sentences could be reconstructed still validate (2 more could not be reconstructed from stored text). A live local re-run on 2026-11-02 under the new prompt and validator (793 items, $0.93) published 770 (97.1%), skipped 17 (2.1%: calendars, charts, tables of contents, and three ceremonial items with nothing beyond their title), and failed 6 (0.8%: three NDEP side-column pages, three model errors). Rejected calls cost 14% of the spend, against 57% before. Sonnet was reached 14 times ($0.19, 20% of spend) and rescued 8; the maintainer kept the fallback.

## Update 2026-11-03: validator 3 and prompt P1

**Maintainer decisions.** A study of stored rejections found that 61 of 113 rejected replies were faithful sentences the validator refused, and that it accepted some unfaithful ones: "$1,267,300" trimmed to "$1,300", "3.25%" to "3%", "2026-2031" to "2026". The maintainer approved all of the following (`VALIDATOR_VERSION` 3):

- *Alignment.* A sentence is matched to its passage with as many words as possible and then as few inner cuts as possible, so an early "the" no longer anchors it in the wrong clause.
- *Numbers and words.* Digits and letters are separate tokens ("2027secured" is two words), a printed number with several digit groups is kept whole or not at all, and lengths and cuts count printed words, so "26-7306-6574-RA (10392)" is two words, not five.
- *Negations.* A dropped negation is allowed when it belongs to a phrase set off from the kept words: a trailing clause after punctuation or starting with "with", "including", "and", "or", or "but" ("..., with no change to the term"); a bracketed aside ("[no County match]"); or a contrast set off by commas or dashes that starts with the negation ("approval, not denial, of"). "Unless", "except", "neither", and "nor" are never dropped, and nor is a negation before the kept words or in a relative clause ("the project, which was not budgeted, ...").
- *Conditions and attribution.* An inner cut may not remove "recommends", "recommended", "requests", "proposes", "proposed", "if", "unless", "should", "may", "might", "would", or "could" (and their close forms). Cutting a sentence's end, or an opening condition up to its comma, is still allowed.
- *Sentence starts.* A passage may start after a short or capitalized heading and a dash ("HEARING - Recorder-Clerk's Office recommending ..."), at the list marker its source begins with ("d. Approve the Project"), or after a footnote asterisk; never inside a word or number.
- *Parentheticals.* Cutting one bracketed or parenthetical group ("Agreement (Amendment No. 3)") does not count against the three inner cuts.
- *Source search.* A copied source with a stray word or two is matched from every run of matching words, not only the longest. A source that starts mid-sentence is extended back to its sentence's start and the sentence checked against the whole sentence.
- *Locating the passage.* When the cited source is what failed (missing or wrong page, not found, mid-sentence, too short or long, or the sentence is not a trim of it), the validator looks on every page, the cited one first, for a report sentence that the summary sentence is a valid trim of under the same rules, and cites that passage: the citation quotes the page's own text, never the model's source, so the export's quote check holds. A sentence that is too long, adds too many words, keeps part of a number, or drops a negation is not looked for elsewhere.
- *Prompt P1* (`prompts/staff_report_summary.md`): keep the report's own subject, verb, and form (never "The Board is recommended to approve", "Staff recommends", or an added "would"), never correct its spelling or grammar, keep a leading "That" or "BE IT RESOLVED, that", and never build a sentence from table cells, column headings, or form labels. Opening conditions ("If approved, ...") may still be left off.

The prompt change re-summarizes every item once (reason `prompt`); see the measurements for the backlog.

**Offline replay** (no API, a snapshot of production's 930 items). Of 113 stored rejected replies, 61 now publish. Of the 902 published summaries the old validator still accepts, one is now rejected (its trim cut the aside "– using boundaries proposed in November 2025 –", arguable) and one loses its second sentence (it cut "if accepted by the Board"); one that the old validator rejected now passes. Hand-written faithful and unfaithful trims, and representative rescued cases, are unit tests in `tests/test_staff_reports.py`.

**Live measurements** (local, no database, 2026-11-03; the shipped validator and prompt). All 930 items, Haiku 5.5 at low effort: the first answer passed or skipped for 900 (96.8%), the retry with the validator's objection rescued 25 of 30, and 5 failed twice, all Nevada water board (NDEP) form pages whose side columns interleave the text, a text-extraction problem handled separately. Result: 909 published, 16 skipped. Cost $0.78, $0.00084 an item including retries. On the 80-item hard set used in the study, 69 (86%) passed first, against 66–74% under the old validator and prompt. Two more runs over the hardest 97 items found one further double failure (a Washoe planning item). First-attempt rejections were mostly sentences starting mid-sentence (9), added words (5), length (5), cuts (3), cut-out conditions (3), and short sources (3).

**Retry chain.** The maintainer asked whether a third Haiku attempt or Opus 5.5 should replace the Sonnet fallback. Retries carry the validator's objection, so they are corrections, not fresh samples; the second Haiku attempt rescued 25 of 26 non-NDEP first-attempt rejections, so the final fallback is reached by about 0.5% of items, and by under 0.2% outside the NDEP form pages. In the study's runs under the new validator, a third Haiku attempt rescued 0 of 4 (all NDEP), and Sonnet rescued 2 of 23 (two NDEP pages whose text is readable; the rest were the four unfixable pages). Opus could not be measured: the workspace's API usage limit was reached mid-experiment. At about 500 items a month the fallback costs pennies whichever model it is (Haiku $0.0011, Sonnet about $0.010, Opus an estimated $0.02 a call; about 3 calls a month), so the choice is about rescue rate. The chain stayed Haiku twice, then Sonnet once, until Opus was measured.

**Opus measurement and change (2026-11-03).** With the usage limit raised, a full run of the 930 items on current code (including the column-aligned page text of 0d0562d) found 26 first-attempt rejections; the second Haiku attempt rescued 24, and 2 items (0.2%) failed twice. No NDEP item reached the fallback any more. On those two items, three replicates each: Opus 5.5 at low effort rescued 6 of 6 ($0.025 a call, 6.6 s median), Sonnet 5.5 at medium 3 of 6 ($0.009), a third Haiku 5 of 6 (one answer cut off at the token limit), and Opus at medium 2 of 2 at about twice the cost of low. In a larger synthetic setting (each candidate given the objection to Haiku's first rejected answer, 28 item and failure-kind cases, two replicates), Opus low rescued 52 of 56 (93%), Sonnet 42 of 56 (75%), mostly failing on length and cuts. Read against their sources, no rescued summary was unfaithful; Opus's were slightly more informative (exact figures where the others rounded). The samples are small and replicates are not independent. At about 500 items a month and 0.2–0.5% reaching the fallback, Opus costs about $0.06 a month against Sonnet's $0.025. The maintainer chose Opus 5.5 at low effort as the final fallback.

**Backlog.** The roughly 930 items in a recent snapshot of production are re-summarized once at about $0.80. The per-run limit (`--summary-max-cost` $0.50, selected by estimate) takes about 600–750 items, and the 45-minute time budget about 500–650 after the earlier steps, so the backlog takes two or three scheduled runs (12–18 hours) without raising the limit. It does count against the shared $5 monthly AI budget, which was more than half spent by 2026-11-03; published summaries keep showing until each item is redone.

## Alternatives and consequences

- Paraphrased summaries read more smoothly but cannot be checked mechanically and drift from the record; the maintainer chose verbatim.
- Letting Claude find the requested action would cost more and add a failure mode for text that is already printed.
- Agencies without a rule keep their agenda excerpt or requested action. A staff report replaced at the same URL without a new agenda is not noticed until the agenda changes.

## Revisit when

- Validation failure rates rise above about 10%, or readers report a misleading trimmed sentence.
- Another agency's agendas or packets expose per-item reports or attachments in a verifiable way.
