# 0024: Verbatim item summaries from requested actions and staff reports

Status: accepted (the verbatim rule and the requested-action rule are maintainer instructions; the 2026-11-02 validator, prompt, partial-summary, and reading-view changes were approved by the maintainer); the maintainer chose on 2026-11-02 to keep the Sonnet fallback after the measured re-run; proposed default for the rest
Date: 2026-10-27
Related ideas: IDEA-013, IDEA-016
Related decisions: 0004, 0010, 0011, 0015, 0020

## Context

An expanded agenda item showed only an agenda excerpt, which usually repeated the title (IDEA-016). The fuller account is in the item's requested action, which many agendas print under the item, and in its staff report or executive summary, which agencies publish separately or inside the agenda packet (IDEA-013).

## Decision

**Accepted maintainer instructions (2026-10-27):**

- **A summary repeats the source verbatim as much as possible**, changing it only where needed for brevity. Sentences are selected from the agenda and the staff report and trimmed; nothing is paraphrased, characterized, or added.
- **The summary leads with the item's requested or recommended action, verbatim, whenever the agenda states one** ("Requested Action / Suggested Motions:", "Recommended Action:", "Recommendation:", "Requested Action:", "Action Requested:"). An item with a printed requested action but no mapped staff report still gets a summary from that text alone. A TTD-style "For Possible Action:" line is the item's title and is not repeated.
- **Show links to every attachment the agenda lists under an item**, labeled from the printed file name, with the printed name kept for matching. No file type is shown: printed extensions are unreliable (South Lake Tahoe serves its .docx attachments as PDFs), and the maintainer found the labels not useful (2026-10-27).

**Proposed defaults (this implementation):**

1. *Staff report links* (`tahoe-ingest map-staff-reports`, `tahoe_ingest/staff_reports.py`). One rule per agency, each requiring two independent signals to agree:
   - South Lake Tahoe: the agenda PDF's "Item N- Executive Summary.pdf" link, and that file's first page reading "Agenda Item #:N" with the item's title and meeting date.
   - TTD: the agenda's Page column, the packet's "~ Page N ~" stamp on that page, and the board's "AGENDA ITEM: N." footer on it.
   - Truckee: the agenda packet (stored, or Municode's MEET-Packet file beside the captured agenda PDF, which must open with that agenda), with a single run of pages headed "Item N.N" whose first page carries the item's title.
   - Lahontan: the agenda's `itemN.pdf` link, headed "ITEM N" with the item's title and meeting.
   Uncertain items store nothing. Only the URL, page range, hash, evidence, and the text of the report's first three pages (for summary citations) are kept; the PDFs are temporary (decision 0004). The meeting page shows "Read the staff report" (or "Read the executive summary") beside "Read in the agenda".
2. *Attachment lists* (South Lake Tahoe). Every link between an item's "N." heading and the next left-margin heading of the agenda PDF belongs to that item, in agenda order. Each printed name must also appear on that page of the stored agenda text, and an executive summary numbered for another item makes the whole list uncertain. URL, printed name, and readable label are stored, never the files.
3. *Requested actions* (`tahoe_ingest/requested_actions.py`, no model). The label must start a line between the item's cited title and the next item's in the same agenda; the action must be one complete statement ending in a period, 5–120 words. It is the exact page text, cited to its page, and is computed at export time from stored text (no extra queries).
4. *Staff report sentences* (`tahoe-ingest summarize-items`, `tahoe_ingest/item_summaries.py`, prompt `prompts/staff_report_summary.md`). Haiku 5.5 at low effort selects one to three sentences (one or two after a printed requested action), each with the cited page and the passage it trimmed. It leads with what is decided or presented when the report says so in at least five words, and never uses procedural boilerplate ("Receive and file", "For presentation only", "No financial impact") as a sentence; for a proclamation, award, or presentation it uses the sentence that says what is recognized (2026-11-02, replacing the rule that always led with the recommendation). A rejected answer is retried with the rejected sentence, its source, the page text near it, and a fix for that kind of rejection; Haiku gets a second try, then Sonnet 5.5 one. Procedural items (roll call, minutes, adjournment, public comment, closed session) are skipped.
5. *Validation before publication* (decision 0010; no human approval). Each passage must be found on its cited page (words compared, so line breaks and misread quote characters do not matter), and each sentence must be that passage with words deleted: at most one inserted "the", "a", "an", or "and" per ten words, no new words or numbers, at most three cuts from the middle of a passage of at most 12 words each, and no dropped negation within or next to the kept words. Each passage must begin where a sentence begins on its page, and a sentence may drop only whole sentences before it, an opening clause ending in a comma or colon, or a list marker such as "(1)", so every published sentence is a whole sentence of the source; cutting the end is free. A source must have 5 to 160 words, and a short or long one is reported as such. The first sentence must pass or nothing is published and the item keeps its agenda text; a later sentence that fails is left out and the rest published (2026-11-02; before, one failing sentence failed the summary). Failed and skipped inputs are recorded and not sent again until something the model sees changes. Each rejected reply is stored with its usage row (`ai_usage.error`, after `reply:`), in the same write, so validator changes can be checked offline. Each result records `VALIDATOR_VERSION` (`item_summaries.py`); raising it after loosening a rule sends the items that failed validation once more, and only those, where a prompt change would send every item. Each run reports why its items are pending (`itemsPendingByReason`): `new`, `prompt`, `report` (the staff report changed), `agenda` (the item was revised), `other` (another part of the message, such as a title or the requested action), `validator`, or `forced`.
   *Reading view* (`reading_view.py`, 2026-11-02). Model and validator read each page in reading order rather than as `pdftotext -layout` prints it: on a form page, labels printed in a side column (the Nevada water board's "System Infrastructure", "for the project") move onto their own lines out of the sentences beside them, and a word hyphenated across a line break is joined when the report prints the joined word elsewhere. Stored page text is not rewritten; a citation quotes the stored page's own span covering the passage (labels moved out of it included), so the export's quote check, the website, staff report mapping, and source fingerprints are unaffected. The label rule is deliberately narrow (a body column at least eight characters in, short labels to its left whose body runs on at that column, and at least two such blocks or three labels on the page); on local data it changes 11 of 1,697 stored report pages (Nevada water board forms, Lahontan timelines, one transit table). Labels printed flush against the body with no consistent column (some NDEP project narratives) are not separated.
6. *Precedence and invalidation.* The export shows the staff report summary while it matches the item's source fingerprint and its current staff report hash; otherwise the agenda's requested action; otherwise the agenda excerpt. A revised agenda changes the fingerprint, so a stale summary is never shown; operator corrections and suppression (`editorial`) apply on top and survive reprocessing. Published summaries are also appended to `summary_versions` (internal audit).
7. *Budget.* Summaries have their own per-run limit (`--summary-max-cost`, default $0.50) under the shared $5 monthly AI budget; a run takes as many pending items as fit and leaves the rest for the next. At most 6 meetings are scanned for staff reports per run (`--staff-report-meetings`), nearest to today first. Mapping writes one statement per scanned meeting and summaries one per meeting; the export reads all of it in one query (13 statements in total, still fixed).

## Measurements (local data, 2026-10-27)

- Staff report links: 135 items in 18 meetings (South Lake Tahoe 60, TTD 36, Truckee 21, Lahontan 18). SLT attachment lists: 75 items, 364 files; one item was left out because a wrapped file name did not match the agenda text.
- Summaries: 117 from staff reports (SLT 53, TTD 30, Truckee 19, Lahontan 15), 42 of them led by the agenda's printed requested action; 40 more items show the agenda's requested action alone (SLT City Council 17, Planning Commission 10, Parks and Recreation Commission 4, Truckee 9). TTD's agenda lines are "For Possible Action:" titles, so its actions come from the staff memos.
- Validation, final rules: of 135 items, 117 published, 8 were skipped by the model (enclosure covers, calendars, or nothing beyond the requested action), and 10 failed validation and kept their agenda text (7%). Before the whole-sentence rule, 1 of 135 failed, but several published sentences were verbatim fragments.
- Cost: about $0.0015 per item with Haiku 5.5 (Sonnet 5.5 fallbacks included). These four agencies add roughly 60–150 items a month: $0.10–0.25 a month.
- Database statements per scheduled run: about 4 reads for each of the two steps, plus about 2 per meeting scanned (at most 6) and 1 per meeting summarized; about 15 on an ordinary run, under 2,000 a month (1% of the free quota).

## Measurements (local data, 2026-11-02)

- Under the rules of 2026-10-27, local runs on 2026-10-28 and 2026-10-29 tried 838 items: Haiku's first answer passed for 616 (74%), its retry rescued 102 of 222 (46%), Sonnet 25 of 120 (21%), and 95 failed (11.3%; 90 of 778 stored items). Failed calls cost $1.02 of $1.78; Sonnet's 120 calls cost $0.90 (half the spend) for 25 summaries. Retries mostly repeated the same rejection, because most rejections were the validator's, not the model's.
- Causes, each reproduced against stored page text: a source under five words ("Action: Receive presentation", "For presentation only.", "No financial impact.") was reported as "not on page"; a passage opening with "$", "(", or a quotation mark could never start a sentence (no published summary had a sentence starting with "$"); only a passage's first occurrence was tried, so a recommended motion that repeats the item's title (every Douglas County cover page) was judged mid-sentence; numbered motions ("3)"), Word's Symbol-font bullet, and "o" sub-bullets did not start a passage; dropping a whole earlier sentence was refused; "Project No. 0184" and a following "No action is required." counted as dropped negations; side-column labels interleaved into sentences (Nevada water board forms).
- After the changes, all 670 published summaries whose sentences could be reconstructed still validate (2 more could not be reconstructed from stored text). A live local re-run on 2026-11-02 under the new prompt and validator (793 items, $0.93) published 770 (97.1%), skipped 17 (2.1%: calendars, charts, tables of contents, and three ceremonial items with nothing beyond their title), and failed 6 (0.8%: three NDEP side-column pages, three model errors). Rejected calls cost 14% of the spend, against 57% before. Sonnet was reached 14 times ($0.19, 20% of spend) and rescued 8; the maintainer kept the fallback.

## Alternatives and consequences

- Paraphrased summaries read more smoothly but cannot be checked mechanically and drift from the record; the maintainer chose verbatim.
- Letting Claude find the requested action would cost more and add a failure mode for text that is already printed.
- Agencies without a rule keep their agenda excerpt or requested action. A staff report replaced at the same URL without a new agenda is not noticed until the agenda changes.

## Revisit when

- Validation failure rates rise above about 10%, or readers report a misleading trimmed sentence.
- Another agency's agendas or packets expose per-item reports or attachments in a verifiable way.
